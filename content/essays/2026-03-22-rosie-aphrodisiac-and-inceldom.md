rosie, aphrodisiac, the death of diet coke, and the prevail of inceldom

Inspired by Jonathan Swift’s A Modest Proposal



My fellow dwellers of the century twenty-first:



“Junkies” who abuse substances have a counterintuitive amount of solidarity when it comes to getting high together, and watching over their peers. For such a deviant system, its participants demonstrate much collaboration and human virtue on a purely mutual basis. 



And, as one of the most heated takes I would ever fathom, I posit that, from a fully pragmatic standpoint of human wellbeing, the phenomenon of victimized empathy may be and ought to be extrapolated to love.



Modern-day dating is increasingly antagonizing. We are pitched to someone who evaluates all that is quantifiable of you and critiques them, and vice versa. Everything you have—childhood trauma, house by the beach, 2017 Honda Civic Type R, halfway decent looks—are weaponized to dehumanize and compare you to other potential “options” that can number to hundreds with the advent of online dating platforms. We look not for love, but to “score” someone out of our “league.” We care for others and show affection on a basis that they put in equal parts of effort, are more attractive, or financially more capable. Love is a social status, a symbol of success, and an assertion of our increasingly inflated egos.



With this comes Incels. Those who aren’t favored by this system. To whom the prospect of finding a partner seems extremely unlikely. They are the victims-turned perpetrators who radicalize their views to sexism, extremism, and outright misogyny, in male cases. From this derives terms like “femcel,” or female Incels, “truecel,” an Incel who has never experienced any physical affection, and the list goes on. Following several high-profile attacks targeting what Incels see as "conventionally attractive” individuals in the 2010s and 2020s brought this marginalized self-identifying group into the mainstream. They are the failures of this system made visible, and a natural outcome of a competitive system that creates permanent losers.



While not to justify their actions, they prove a larger trend, which is that the repressive ways of finding affection victimizes individuals of lower social standing rather than mutually benefit both parties, as it was designed to do when it had been purely for procreation. To protect one another and to care as much in familial bonds as in romantic ones. We have lost that ability to care independent of social and societal factors, to love magnanimously without demanding that something be returned to us.



Since love no longer functions in these facilities largely under this prevalent system, it is futile to promote self-improvement in order to conform to the system as a whole. The act of improving one’s physical appearance or financial standing solely to receive sexual attraction intrinsically estranges one from others because it accepts the dehumanizing view that love and sexual affection are a competition, and everyone is either ahead or behind. This is extensively depicted in the experience of Clavicular, an internet figure who became characterized by their extreme pursuit of physical looks and “mogging,” or having better appearances than others.



In sensational pop icon Rose’s debut album Rosie, she writes, In the track “3am” and “number one girl,”

“I just saw a red flag…gonna pretend I didn’t see that…I need you really, really that bad…The one I pretty-talk and ugly-cry to, when it’s 3am…the world can roll their eyes.” 



“I’d do anything to make you want me, I’d give it all up if you told me, I’d be the number one girl in your eyes…”



This encapsulates the current situation of love in contemporary art. We are faced with either the option of conforming to the sensible comparisons and rationale of competitive dating, or the precarious, toxic nature of pursuing someone out of instinct notwithstanding all their flaws and incompatibilities. Specifically, she mentions how the person she “needs” is someone that the world would “roll their eyes to” suggesting social disapproval clashing with intrinsic desire. Picking between the two is comparable to choosing the color of your shoelaces while walking into a two-way eight lane highway of oncoming traffic. We run no middle ground because we believe foolishly that love should be hard to come by and hard to maintain, to merit its fruit. That’s like saying dopamine only exists when you live your fullest when you can just do meth. We simply refuse to acknowledge that love, along with sexual pleasure which is already exploited in hookup culture, is also exploitable through psychological tweaks in our neural pathways. 



So to say, we already treat attraction as something that can be optimized, curated, and, when necessary, hacked. We adjust lighting, bodies, personalities, even our speech patterns to increase desirability. Entire industries exist to manufacture appeal. The only boundary we still pretend to respect is that affection must remain “authentic”—that it must originate naturally, unprovoked. But this distinction is already eroding. If attraction can be engineered indirectly, there is no principled reason dictating it cannot be engineered directly. The mutual, neurological engineering to eradicate the social and, subsequently, equivocal and oppressive facets of affection and intimacy back to its most rudimentary form is therefore necessarily the logical endpoint of our evolution in mating.



Since the inevitable randomness and sporadic, inefficient nature of love has caused real deaths and wellbeing issues in many others, it necessarily follows that the next iteration of intimacy will not rely on chance or competition at all.



So, I propose that if there exists to be such a drug, such that it is similar to an aphrodisiac but simultaneously substantially stimulates our drive to care and to show empathy to others regardless of their physical and social standing—like is seen in some methamphetamines and MDMA, or synthetic peptide hormones like bremelanotide given to treat hypoactive sexual desires (HSDD)—and, given that it is not overly hindering or addictive, we should administer them to those in difficulty of finding love in presence of similar participants en masse as part of a co-operative with the aim obviate the need for traditional relationships. Not as an ill-advised sort of authoritarian breeding chamber, but an emotional reconciliation through induced promiscuity. 



We hurt others by our reluctance to care and sympathize for those we deem “a bad look” or societally unacceptable. By forming these essentially chemically-driven polycules, we’re fostering the mutual and truthful bonds that humanity has relied on for so long, before our estrangements with one another.



To illustrate my point, I will use the example of Diet Coke. I am fairly certain that there are not many people who drink diet coke purely for taste. For soda drinkers, it is very common to have fallen to the allure of sugary carbonated drinks like regular coke, Sprite, or Fanta, or Mountain Dew (Dr. Pepper, god forbid) only to eventually default to Diet Coke out of the supposed health risks that regular soda posits. I hardly ever drink regular soda now, in spite of all my desires. Simply because diet coke offers a halfway decent replacement to an obviously flawed choice.



Many see diet coke as a compromise, an alternative that naturally falls short because it can never be the option it aims to replace. Except, sometimes the alternative offers a completely different way to see things. The automobile was an expensive, impractical alternative to horse carriages at first. It lacked warmth, familiarity, even a certain romance.We cannot pat our cars on the head or enjoy the bond of feeding it, but that did not matter in time. The car did not become a better horse. It made the horse irrelevant.



 So what if we are approaching that same threshold with love?

What if what we call romance—monogamy, chemistry, the slow choreography of mutual choice or, more often, selection by societal standards—shouldn’t be refined, but entirely overhauled? Replaced not by something colder, but more engineered. More reliable. Less dependent on chance and selfish whims. Irreverent to the chains that our social standing binds us to.

So I am suggesting an initial alternative to regular forms of love, and, eventually, a complete subversion of monogamy. Except, in place of diet coke, I’m suggesting government-sanctioned polycules formed by mass injections of aphrodisiac as the future groundstone of our communities instead of our conventional ways of finding love, sex, and companionship.



Yet, one may well argue still that the act of bringing individuals who seek monogamy or a conventional relationship into this forced, psychedelic state is unethical and insufficient. But whilst polyamory may not be adopted universally, this new mode of attaining physical and sexual intimacy needs not to emulate the outdated modes of monogamy. In fact, it needs only to be an apparition of human love, an emulation of pure affection. This facility would operate with everyone as part of the co-operative by, out of good nature and aphrodisiac, accepting requests for intimacy, sexual or otherwise, and vice versa. An anarchist potluck of love, sex, and messed up human feelings. 



This would not share any downsides that conventional intimacy fatally suffers from. The structure removes the central risk of intimacy—exclusion—by design. Instead of entrusting your own affection and wellbeing on the potentially unrequited opinion of another, this hedges the risk of being “unwanted” or feeling lonely altogether. Everyone belongs to everyone else, much like the system in Brave New World. Essentially, everyone is up for fondling, and so are you to everyone else. But not out of selfish desires of possession or to subdue, but rather a shared altruistic vision to pleasure and serve the community as equals. 



Further, if such a system is completely voluntary, it falls into the category similar to victim help groups or community-run mental health sessions. Just as there will never be any law to prevent the usage of horsecars, there’d be no enforcement of this natural iteration. But there simply would not need to be. In our current political, economical, and social systems, this is the most optimized way to attain human requirements for love and intimacy. Participation would be fluidly normalized with minimal legislation, just as editing your photos to quasi-deceive strangers online was.



My friends, you see I profit none from this system. For there is no money to be made, nor exploits to be extorted in this co-operative. I propose this only for the humble betterment of us less-advantaged, of the lonely and of a dire, yet poignantly pointed resistance towards this atomized society. We shall heal human hearts, one orgy at a time.
